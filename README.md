# Monitor arm for the Sceptre C32 (C325B-185RD1)

A 3D-printed desk-clamp arm for the Sceptre C32 240 Hz curved monitor. It screws into the four M4 inserts on the monitor's back and holds its 5.26 kg, with a bracket curved to sit flat on the 1800R housing. It adjusts height, swivel, reach, pan, tilt and rotation.

`step/monitor_arm_assembly.step` is the full arm, bolts included. `step/` and `stl/` hold each printed part.

You also need the stand's two M4 × 12 screws, an old bike inner tube for rubber, and a borrowed 6.5 mm drill bit.

## Printing

PETG, 0.2 mm layers, no supports, about 1.35 kg total. Desk clamp: 8 walls, 40% infill. Other parts: 4 to 6 walls, 15 to 20% infill. Print the three thin washers solid.

## Assembly

1. Press an M10 lock nut into the clamp's lower jaw, thread the M10 × 100 up through it, and glue on the knob and pad. Clamp it with the pole 45 mm in from the desk's back edge.
2. Push the pipe into the socket and lock it with an M6 screw.
3. Mark the pipe through the collar's side hole, drill a 6.5 mm hole through the pipe, and pin the collar with the ¼" bolt.
4. Slide link 1 onto the pipe. The second M6 screw sets how stiffly it swings.
5. Bolt link 2 on top of link 1, and the head under link 2. Each joint gets an M10 × 120, a printed washer between the parts and fender washers at both ends.
6. Bolt the bracket to the tilt block with the M8 × 30, add the M5 rotation lock, then screw the bracket to the monitor with four M4 × 12 screws.
7. Hang the tilt block in the yoke on the M8 × 70, stacked: washer, ear, rubber, tongue, rubber, ear, 3 disc springs (alternating), washer, lock nut. Tighten until it holds 5° forward.
8. Leave it at full reach for 24 hours, then retighten every nut.
